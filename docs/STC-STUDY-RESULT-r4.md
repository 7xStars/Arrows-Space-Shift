The user found the r3 techno study closer to the target.

Revision r4 changes the instruments and percussion.
It does not increase the master or bus defaults.

This is a design judgment, not a measured player result.

The pilot tests that direction before a full campaign music pass.

## Implemented study

The original score uses 40 bars at 134 BPM.
Its musical form lasts about 71.64 seconds.
The WAV adds a short ending tail.
The acid sequence retains its identity through accents, slides, and filter changes.
Interlocking percussion and a displaced cycle add rhythmic movement.
The form removes layers before their return.
Revision r4 adds kick punch, snare body, tom fills, and selected half-step accents.
It reduces the acid resonance and square-wave content.
Controlled drive and a post-filter change the synth texture.

The playable field has five phases.
It retains one physical attack through actual Bolza crossings.
The source motif precedes the return and keeps the same object identity.
The transfer example changes the start and heading.
The first choice is recorded before destination feedback.
Earlier choices, guide use, condition order, and retries remain in the exported record.
Audio-only mode is unscored listening. It is not blind combat.
The study counts contacts without hull loss or campaign rewards.
Its mix controls remain in the current session.
It does not write campaign storage.

The score form has its own audio clock.
The player can advance geometry phases at a different rate.
Actual entry and crossing events provide the live cue connection.
This pilot does not establish complete phase synchronization or a causal learning benefit.
