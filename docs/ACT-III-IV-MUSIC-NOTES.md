# Act III and Act IV music definitions

Source: `audio/stc-act-library-r1/act34.json`.
Role: Music Composition.
Status: Composition data is ready for rendering. Listening approval remains open.

The eight definitions use the same data contract as the other Act library pieces.
Each Act has a 60-second, 120-second, 240-second, and 360-second piece.
The definitions total 26 minutes.
They are separate compositions. Their motifs, percussion grids, harmonic material, and forms differ.

## Act III

| Piece | Length | Main experiment |
|---|---|---|
| Iron Intake | 1 minute | Establish a bass call. Add a mechanical response. Remove it before the return. |
| After the Release | 2 minutes | Keep root-and-fifth motion while harmony and atmosphere create a recovery interval. |
| Order of Force | 4 minutes | Compare reverse-then-rotate with rotate-then-reverse over the same pulse. |
| Machinery of Return | 6 minutes | Develop pressure, open harmony, withheld layers, and a later return. |

The rhythm has an EBM and industrial role.
The bass stays in the foreground.
The shorter probe limits harmony to expose the rhythmic relation.
The longer pieces add harmonic recovery rather than a permanent increase in density.
The harmonic root cycle in the second and fourth pieces is F, B-flat, E-flat, and F.
The bass uses roots, fifths, and octaves across that cycle.

## Act IV

| Piece | Length | Main experiment |
|---|---|---|
| Paired Horizon | 1 minute | Retain a phrase while its upper range moves between stereo positions. |
| Quiet Curvature | 2 minutes | Hold a G pedal while irregular atmosphere and upper harmony change. |
| Order Leaves a Trace | 4 minutes | Compare ordered motif operations while chord pitch classes remain fixed. |
| The Surface Remembers | 6 minutes | Carry a bass cell through discovery, pressure, quiet space, and harmonic return. |

Act IV retains a pulse during its sparse sections.
Its atmosphere has a longer timescale than the percussion.
The third piece uses four voicings of pitch classes F, A-flat, C, and G.
The voicings change register and order. Their pitch-class set remains equal.
This is a musical relation. It is not a model of Bolza side pairing.
