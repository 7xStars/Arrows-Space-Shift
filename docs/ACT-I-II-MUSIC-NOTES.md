## Musical distinctions

Act I uses electro accents, clear entrances, and restrained harmony.
Its first piece has no sustained pads in its form.
The second piece introduces sparse seventh and ninth voicings.
The third piece isolates operation order around a stable pitch collection.
The long piece adds harmonic movement and two recovery sections.

Act II retains a more constant kick and a stronger acid identity.
Its upper-bass pan depth increases while the low foundation remains the render contract's responsibility.
Its first piece isolates a reversal without a sustained chord bed.
The second adds atmosphere around two related minor harmonic regions.
The third holds the harmonic root still while operation order changes.
The long piece moves between related harmonic regions and restores its original cell after recovery.

Each piece has its own bass motif, drum pattern, seed, form, and mix parameters.
The longer pieces are not duration extensions of the short piece.
Tempo changes do not stand for an increase in gameplay difficulty.

## Structural limits

Reversal and rotation are musical sequence operations.
They do not compute a Klein identification or a hyperbolic isometry.
A voicing change can preserve pitch classes within the same chord.
A harmonic progression can change those pitch classes.
These two operations must not be described as the same invariant.

The `hypothesis` fields are questions for later listening.
They are not results or ratings.
No listener recognition rate, emotional response, or gameplay comprehension has been measured.
The musical section labels do not schedule enemies or warn of an attack.
The game retains control of physics, collision, and actual warning events.

The English uses short sentences and consistent technical terms.
Full ASD-STE100 conformity and complete dictionary review are not claimed.
