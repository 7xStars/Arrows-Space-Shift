# Timing course playtest

Candidate: Arrow Space Shift 0.3.
Question: can a player predict and change arrival times, then apply that understanding to a combined threat?
This document is a protocol. It is not a completed playtest report.

## Prepare

Use a keyboard and the normal URL, without `?test=1`.
Record the browser, screen size, prior exposure, and any assistance.
Keep the same candidate, controls, thrust rules, and target behavior for a score comparison.
Choose the first score setting before the session.
Vary that order across players when practical.
A repeat by the same player is a learning-affected comparison.
Do not describe it as another first-use observation.

Open **Playtest notes and results**.
Let the player read the brief and controls.
The first two encounters teach their operation explicitly.
The combined encounter states the goals without supplying the sequence.
Therefore this protocol tests transfer after instruction, not discovery without instruction.

## Observe

Before starting each encounter, ask:

> What do you expect to arrive first? What might your action change?

Record the prediction before the player selects Start.
Do not correct it before the attempt.
Let the player complete or fail the attempt.
Record the controls used, result, assistance, and visible consequences.

After the result, ask:

> What caused that result? What could you change on another attempt?

Keep the answer separate from the observer's interpretation.
Record it in the explanation field.
Save the results before closing or reloading the page.
The export is local. It does not send data to a service.
The game does not record spoken answers or screen video.

## Compare

Repeat with the other score setting.
Use Restart to unlock setup.
Ask what changed in the player's decision, if anything.
Distinguish the effect of score from the effect of practice.
Do not infer enjoyment from points, survival, or completion time alone.

Check these competing explanations:

- The player predicts the shared effect and uses it deliberately.
- The player follows the brief without understanding the cause.
- Repeated input or trial and error produces a success.
- The player understands the rule but cannot act before the deadline.
- The score display changes behavior without improving the spatial decision.

## Stop and review

End the round after a traceable attempt and explanation for each encounter.
Stop earlier if a comprehension or control problem prevents a meaningful attempt.
Identify the smallest correction before adding more content.
Keep the raw failures and prior predictions.
Do not convert a small exploratory round into a reliability or general balance claim.

A completed technical fixture is not a completed human research round.
