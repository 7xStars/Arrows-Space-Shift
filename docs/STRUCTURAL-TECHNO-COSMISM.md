Structural Techno-Cosmism is this project's provisional artistic language.
It is not an established historical genre.
Its purpose is to make spatial relationships clear through movement and sound.

## Design standards

Simulation truth has first priority.
Threat visibility has second priority.
Control feedback follows these requirements.
Decorative effects must preserve them.

| Principle | Current application | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Repetition | Original score patterns, weapon cues, and attack motifs repeat | Recognition is not measured |
| Identity | Route and boss shots retain presentation IDs through actual crossings | Sound recognition is not measured |
| Transformation | Crossing sounds differ by the actual rule | Sound is not the geometry calculation |
| Phase | Actual warnings, boss phase information, and score variations mark change | Music does not schedule attacks |
| Disruption | Damage and phase cues mark real changes | No random full-screen glitch effect is added |
| Empty space | The black starfield, sparse recovery score, and quiet intervals remain | Musical balance has no listening acceptance |

Electro, hypnotic techno, industrial music, EBM, acid techno, and ambient music guide the small runtime arrangements.
Act I uses syncopation and mechanical accents.
Act II uses repeated pulse and resonant bass filtering.
Act III uses a square-bass mechanical arrangement.
Act IV uses sparse pulse, motif fragments, and a quiet drone.
Recovery and ending have separate arrangements.
These are original procedural compositions in `src/music.js`.
Their genre influences are creative intentions. No listening review has accepted their quality.
The project's source context includes Detroit techno, electro, and Black Afrofuturist traditions.
This context does not authorize the use of commercial recordings.

## Implemented boundary

The current update uses an immutable event registry and actual sound routing.
It connects Act IV warnings and crossing events to the audio system.
It adds independent gain buses, bounded noise and soft-distortion caches, priority-based voice replacement, and gesture recovery.
It preserves manual firing and existing topology calculations.
It retains most existing combat animation.
The Double Torus view adds phase, hull, and recovery information.
The dock's repair sequence combines manual fire, a fold, a Klein return, and capture/release.
Its ending shows a compressed continuous route and ship departure.
The Act IV view retains its separate renderer.

The complete proposed shared animation engine and optional research encounter are not delivered here.
Exported production stems and an A/B/C workstation comparison are not part of this slice.
The operator examples in the PDF remain proposed analogies.
They are not proofs about player learning or game geometry.
The hybrid Klein/projective encounter remains experimental.
The finite Bolza collision search remains an approximation.
